The document is not shippable at 1736 lines - roughly 800 of them are review narrative, measurement logs and arguments with its own drafts; I would defend 550.
Gate 1 can still rule on Q1 today: the fork argument is intact and verified byte-identical on both consumer surfaces.

## Job A: round-2 fold audit

### G1 - a retained list of restart instants

**CLAIM.** §6.2 (lines 663-680): the detector takes "a list of restart instants covering at least the lookback"; `RestartBudget._stamps` (`supervision.py:114`) "already keeps them" but prunes on `restart_window` (1800s), so the list must be retained for `max(restart_window, lookback)`; it rides on `SupervisorState`.

**WHAT I RAN.**

```
grep -n "_stamps" bobi/supervisor/supervision.py          # 1008r3-main
grep -n "_budget\." bobi/supervisor/*.py
sed -n '101,141p;455,485p;655,690p' bobi/supervisor/supervision.py
```

Every mechanical half of the claim checks out. `_stamps` is at `supervision.py:114`. `_recent` (`:116-117`) filters on `self.window`; `count()` (`:119-122`) **mutates** `self._stamps = self._recent(now)`, and `_report` calls `count()` every poll (`:787`), so stamps really are pruned to 1800s on every cycle. `_note_restart` really does overwrite a scalar (`supervision.py:424`).

**VERDICT: FIX INTRODUCED A NEW DEFECT.** Three of them.

1. **`_stamps` is not a record of restart instants.** `_operator_restart_manager` calls `self._budget.reset()` (`supervision.py:473`), and `reset()` sets `self._stamps = []` (`:134`) with the comment "An OPERATOR restart is intentional, not a crash/wedge, so it must not draw down (or trip) the loop budget." So an operator `restart` kills the child, orphans every in-flight session, **and empties the exclusion's entire input** before `_respawn()`. The 08-03 reap is as consistent with an operator bounce as with a crash restart, and under the fold that case opens the spurious incident.
2. **The transient-crash relaunch records nothing.** `_handle_child_exit` calls `self._budget.record(now)` only on the `fast_crash` branch (`:666`). The non-fast-crash branch (`:678-682`) calls `_respawn()` and `_note_restart("crash", now, charged=False)` with **no** `record()`. So a child that ran healthy for >120s and then died is restarted, orphans its sessions, and leaves no stamp.
3. The fold therefore traded a **complete but scalar** signal (`_note_restart` sets `_last_restart_at` on every restart edge, charged or not) for an **incomplete list**. That is a coverage regression, not a repair.

Separately, **retaining `_stamps` longer changes the restart budget.** `count()` prunes and counts in one method and `exhausted()` is `count(now) >= max_restarts` (`:124-126`). Widening `self.window` to 3600s makes `max_restarts=3` apply over an hour instead of 30 minutes - a live behaviour change to the restart machine, which §10 and §8 both promise this spec does not touch ("**Any change to restart behaviour.** None. Explicitly."). The spec's "rather than reused as-is" acknowledges a problem without naming the fix; an implementor following it literally breaks the budget.

On sufficiency of the 300s window: it is sufficient *for the reaps it sees*, and the spec names the >300s reap-latency residual honestly. The hole is upstream of the window, in the input.

**WHAT TO CHANGE.** Delete the apparatus. See Job B: exclude the `DIED_WITHOUT_TERMINAL` signature by name and the measured false positive dies with one comparison and zero new state.

### G2 - the `supervisor_uptime_s` rule

**CLAIM.** §6.2 (lines 691-695): "the rule for that case uses a field already on the object: if `supervisor_uptime_s` is less than the lookback, discard every entry whose `terminal_at` predates this supervisor's boot."

**WHAT I RAN.**

```
grep -rn "supervisor_uptime\|uptime" bobi/supervisor/*.py   # 1008r3-main
sed -n '143,170p;781,795p' bobi/supervisor/supervision.py
```

**The field exists and is on the right object.** `SupervisorState.supervisor_uptime_s: float` at `supervision.py:154`, populated in `_report` as `max(0.0, now - self._started_at)` at `:786`, already published at `snapshot.py:149`. No blocker there.

**VERDICT: HOLDS mechanically, FIX INTRODUCED A NEW DEFECT behaviourally** - and the defect is the G2/G4 interaction the brief asked about.

- **Effect on the true positive.** Benign for the reported outage: §3's director was idle the whole 102 minutes, so no restart path fires. If a supervisor restart did land mid-outage, the cost is latency, not blindness - post-boot failures still count, and at the July window's inter-failure gaps (max 25.5 min, measured) re-reaching N=3 takes 4 to 76 minutes. State that; do not claim it blinds nothing.
- **First hour of every supervisor life.** It does not blind the detector, it blinds the detector's *history*. With a mean of 0.47 dispatches/hour (re-derived) a fresh supervisor usually has fewer than 3 post-boot terminals, so it reads not-failing for most of its first hour. Given `EXIT_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED = 70` (`supervision.py:74`, returned at `:628`, `:664`) deliberately exits into a machine restart, that hour recurs on exactly the crash-loop tail where a brain-fatal cause is most likely. §10 scopes the active-director crash-loop case out (§4.1 F2), which makes it defensible, but the spec does not say the uptime rule is what pays for that scoping.
- **The real defect, G2 x G4.** Walk the first poll after a supervisor replacement with an incident file on disk:
  1. uptime < 3600 -> every pre-boot entry discarded -> the window is empty of usable entries -> `unknown`.
  2. The alerter loads `supervisor-session-incident.json` and reads `last_failing_at`, written by the *previous* process, possibly hours old.
  3. §7.1's abandon test is `unknown` continuously for a lookback. With a stale `last_failing_at` the elapsed test is already satisfied, so **abandon fires on the first poll**, silently, with no RECOVERED notice, while the outage may be ongoing.
  4. The uptime rule then suppresses re-opening for the rest of the hour.

  Net: a supervisor restart during a session-fatal outage destroys the incident record (including the `opened_at` every duration is measured from, §5.2) and goes quiet for up to an hour. The spec never addresses it.
- **Unspecified sub-case.** §7.2 says the file carries `last_failing_at`, but nothing says what happens when it is absent - an incident opened by an older build, or a process death between `_save()` of the open and the first `failing` save (#1097 proved that ordering is live). `now - None` either raises (fail-open to "never abandon", reviving round 1's F2) or defaults to `opened_at` (immediate abandon). Pick one in writing.

**WHAT TO CHANGE.** The uptime rule exists only to cover G1's missing stamps. Delete both with the apparatus; then the abandon clock has no interaction to be incoherent with, because nothing discards entries on a boot boundary.

### G3 - `ok` now requires a success in the window

**CLAIM.** §5.2 line 452: `ok` means "the lookback window contains **at least one success**, and §6.2 does not hold".

**WHAT I RAN.** Re-read §5.2's table, the §6 diagram (lines 585-610), §7.1 (lines 993-1004), §11 tests 2/3/13/16 (lines 1255-1276) in `1008r3-992`; re-implemented the revised condition over all 674 detector-visible terminal events and censused a 30 s poll grid over 75.5 days.

**VERDICT: FIX INTRODUCED A NEW DEFECT.** The definition is sound; three places in the spec were not updated to it, and one pair of them is a direct contradiction.

1. **The §6 diagram still carries the predicate G3 said is unimplementable.** Lines 592-599 label the `failing -> ok` edge "by a session SUCCEEDED and STARTED AFTER the incident". §5.2's definition contains no incident term. G3's whole finding was that a pure registry function cannot evaluate "after the incident opened". The fold bolted a correction note on at lines 601-602 and left the broken edge above it, so the document now ships **two different `ok` predicates**, one of them the one G3 rejected.
2. **Test 2 asserts the wrong state.** Line 1255: "N failures with *different* signatures -> `ok`." Under line 452 a window of failures with no success is **`unknown`**, not `ok`. I confirmed this against my implementation of the revised rule. Test 3 (line 1256, a success interleaved) is correct and still yields `ok`.
3. **§7.1's `ok` claim is false, and test 16(d) contradicts test 13.** Line 1001-1002 says "any non-`unknown` observation resets it: an `ok` closes the incident through the RECOVERED path instead". §5.2's `ok` needs only *a* success in the window; §7.1's CLOSE needs a success that **started after the open**. Those differ precisely in the case §7.1 itself declares real ("a long investigation dispatched before the credential died can complete twenty minutes into the incident"). On that input:
   - §5.2 -> state = `ok`.
   - Test 16(d), line 1276: "A single `ok` observation closes the incident through RECOVERED instead" -> must post RECOVERED.
   - Test 13, line 1268: "A `completed` session that started BEFORE the incident opened does NOT close it" -> must not.

   Two tests in one verification plan demand opposite behaviour on one input. Either resolution is a wrong signal: close -> the false all-clear test 13 exists to prevent; do not close -> the clock is reset every poll by an `ok` that cannot close, so the incident is held open for up to a full lookback with no close path. That is round 1's F2 defect, narrowed but alive.
4. The fold's own strongest sentence overstates. "The success requirement in `ok` is the load-bearing half" removes the *zero-success* false `ok` (1.60% of polls) but not the *pre-incident-success* false `ok`, which is the same contradiction with a different cause.

**WHAT TO CHANGE.** Do not patch the enum; delete it. See Job B - a `load_grace`-shaped null-or-block field has no `ok` to define, no edge to mislabel and no test 16(d).

### G4 - the ABANDON timer

**CLAIM.** §7.1 lines 997-1003: clock starts at the last `failing` poll, persisted as `last_failing_at`, any non-`unknown` observation resets it.

**WHAT I RAN.** Poll-grid census at 30 s over the full registry span.

```
polls: 217367
failing            222   0.10%
ok               35469  16.32%
unknown         181676  83.58%
empty_window    178201  81.98%
non-empty window, ZERO success:  3475   1.60%
```

**VERDICT: HOLDS on reachability, FIX INTRODUCED A NEW DEFECT on the resets.**

- **Reachability: yes.** 82.0% of polls see an entirely empty one-hour window, and with G3's demotion a failures-only window is `unknown` too, so `unknown` is the resting state and a full lookback of it is routine. G3's fold made G4's path *more* reachable, not less.
- **First poll after a supervisor restart: broken.** Covered in G2 above - a stale `last_failing_at` plus an instant `unknown` from the uptime rule fires abandon on poll one. This is the specific interaction the brief flagged, and it is real.
- **The reset rule is wrong.** "Any non-`unknown` observation resets it" plus "an `ok` closes through RECOVERED" is only coherent if `ok` implies the CLOSE predicate, which G3 shows it does not. An `ok` produced by a pre-incident success resets the clock without being able to close, once per poll, for as long as that success sits in the lookback - up to 120 consecutive polls. Bounded at one lookback window per stale success, so a defect rather than a hang, but the spec asserts an implication that does not hold.
- **Absent `last_failing_at`: unspecified.** As in G2.

**WHAT TO CHANGE.** Make the clock "the condition in §6.2 has not held for a full lookback window". That needs no `unknown` state, no three-value vocabulary and no reset-on-`ok` rule, because it is a property of the condition rather than of a published enum. On §3's own incident it fires at 01:17:05 instead of 02:17:30 - one hour sooner, same operational outcome (silence before the 05:45:08 first provable recovery).

### G6 - `since` split from `opened_at`

**CLAIM.** §5.2 lines 466-477: `since` is re-derived per poll as the oldest failure in the current streak; "**every** duration the alerter reports comes from [the persisted `opened_at`], never from the published `since`".

**WHAT I RAN.** Derived all three candidate durations from the 13 real session-limit entries.

```
open_at (3rd terminal_at)             23:04:15
since = oldest-in-streak terminal_at  22:39:35   -> 24.7 min
oldest-in-streak started_at           22:35:15   -> 29.0 min
last success before the open          22:25:51   -> 38.4 min
```

**VERDICT: FIX INTRODUCED A NEW DEFECT - the split is incomplete.**

§7.3's own example message says "none completed in **29m**". That is `opened_at - oldest streak failure's started_at`. It is **not** derivable from `opened_at` (which is 0 at open time), and it is not the published `since` (24.7 min). So §5.2's absolute rule is contradicted by the spec's own flagship alert text, by a **fourth** quantity the document never names anywhere. Three named quantities, four in use.

Worse, the sentence is also semantically false: "none completed in 29m" reads as time since the last success, which was **38.4 min**, not 29. The alert understates the silence by 9.4 minutes on the spec's own incident.

Test 12 (line 1267, "RECOVERED ... with duration") is consistent with the `opened_at` rule and needs no change.

**WHAT TO CHANGE.** Either drop the duration from the OPEN message (the quoted error is the operator's action; the elapsed figure is decoration) or name the fourth quantity explicitly and fix the wording to "no dispatch has completed in 38m". The first is the cut.

## Job B: the cut

### The minimum design

One paragraph, which is the whole design:

> Once per supervisor cycle, stat `state/sessions/*/state.json` and parse only those whose mtime is inside the last hour. Of the terminal events in that hour, if the most recent **3** are all in `FAILED_STATUSES`, carry the same non-empty first-line error, that error is not `DIED_WITHOUT_TERMINAL`, and the manager reads `running` or `idle`, the condition holds. On its first hold, post one SOFT Slack notice quoting the error verbatim and persist `{opened_at, signature}` to `supervisor-session-incident.json`. Close it with one RECOVERED notice on the first success that *started* after `opened_at`. Clear it silently if the condition has not held for a full hour. Publish `session_health` on the heartbeat as `null` when the condition does not hold, or `{error, since}` when it does, exactly the way `load_grace` is published. One env kill switch.

Verified against the two acceptance criteria over the real registry:

```
N=3, 1h lookback, shared alpha-bearing signature, no success interleaved:
  bare                      holds=14  opens=2   <- the 08-03 reap is open #2
  drop DIED_WITHOUT_TERMINAL holds=11 opens=1   <- 2026-07-30 23:04:15 only
  min-spread >= 60s          holds=11 opens=1   <- same
```

(a) detected: open at `2026-07-30 23:04:15`, signature `you've hit your session limit · resets 12:40am (utc)`, 29 minutes into the 102-minute outage.
(b) not false-positived: the 08-03 cluster never opens, at any poll time, with no restart input of any kind.

**The single rule that kills the 08-03 reap.** Two candidates, both one line, both verified above.

*Name exclusion* - `DIED_WITHOUT_TERMINAL` is not a signature. It is a named constant (`sdk.py:50`) shared by the only two reap sites (`sdk.py:592`, `reconcile.py:132`), and in both it is the **"no error was recorded" sentinel**: `reconcile.py:132` reads `error = entry.error or DIED_WITHOUT_TERMINAL`, and the dispatch path always records real text (`subagent.py:552-555` writes `tool_crash_error(e)` on any `connect()`-time raise). So the string means "this session died and nobody wrote down why", which is the orphan shape and never the brain-fatal shape. What it gives up: a brain-fatal cause that kills the agent process before it can write its error at all - an OOM or a SIGKILL, which is a host condition the crash-loop and load-grace paths already own. I think that give-up is correct.

*Minimum spread* - the N streak entries must span at least 60 s. The registry's spread distribution is bimodal with a 25x gap and nothing in between:

```
smallest spread of any same-signature failed/crashed triple, whole registry:
     1.1s / 6.8s / 9.0s   agent process died without reporting a terminal status
   224.7s and up          you've hit your session limit ...
```

It is strictly the stronger false-positive filter: at N=2 it still yields exactly 1 open where the name exclusion leaves 2 (an `agent exceeded its #s timeout` pair on 08-20), so it would let N drop to 2 and recover the 4 minutes §6.2's table argues about. Its give-up is a real false negative: a burst dispatch (five `wf-pr-closed` runs fired by five PR closes) all dying at `connect()` within seconds would be suppressed, and that is exactly a session-fatal signature.

**I would ship the name exclusion and not the spread rule.** It is safer on the true positive, it needs no new constant, and it names the thing it excludes. If Gate 1 wants both, the spread rule is additive and free later.

What this minimum deletes: the restart-instant list, the 300 s window, the `supervisor_uptime_s` rule, the `ok` state, the `unknown` state, the `state` field, the `failures` field, digit collapse, 120-char truncation, the no-alpha rule's digit half, `supervision.py` and `tests/test_supervision.py` from scope, and three of test 10's clock positions. It changes no answer on either acceptance case.

## Job C: the fork

**What M loses, concretely.** One consumer, and it exists: `bobi/supervisor/admin.py:280` returns `self.telemetry.last_snapshot` verbatim for the admin `status` command, and `telemetry.py:174` assigns `self.last_snapshot = snapshot` from `build_heartbeat`. **Is R's block read by anything?** No. `grep -rn "load_grace"` over the worktree returns 18 hits in `supervision.py`, 18 in `telemetry.py`, 5 in `config.py`, 1 in `snapshot.py` - all producer - plus 26 in tests and 4 in `ADMIN_PROTOCOL.md`. **How much does M save?** 4 of 11 files - `snapshot.py`, `supervision.py`, `docs/ADMIN_PROTOCOL.md`, `tests/test_supervision.py` - because with one consumer there is no two-scan desync to prevent and so no `SupervisorState` field. That is the honest number, and it is smaller than "the floor" implies.

**Is §5.3's argument a point against M?** No - it is a point against the whole spec, and §5.3 should not be allowed to keep it. **H1 - BLOCKER - `RestartBudget._stamps` does not contain the restart instants G1's fold needs.**
